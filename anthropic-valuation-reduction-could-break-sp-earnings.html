A Reduced Valuation of Anthropic Can Materially Hurt Reported S&P 500 Earnings Growth

The accounting path from Anthropic's price to the index headline is already open. It ran upward last quarter, and it can run the other way.

Summary. Amazon and Alphabet booked large, unrealized Anthropic-related gains in Q2 2026. Those gains flowed through GAAP earnings into FactSet's headline S&P 500 growth rate, which printed at 52.0% compared with 33.8% excluding the two companies.

After an IPO, their combined stakes of about $315B would be marked to market through profit every quarter. A modest 10% post-listing decline would take roughly 3.5 to 4 points off headline index growth. That is enough to be material.

The thesis depends on a listing by about Q1 2027. This is a claim about capacity, not a forecast.

Definitions

Reduced valuation
A lower mark the holders actually book, whether from a priced round, an IPO price, or a falling share price after listing. Betting-market or secondary prices count only if they feed a recorded mark.
Can
A realistic possibility under current accounting rules and current holdings, within the next four reporting seasons (Q3 2026 through Q2 2027). Not any conceivable scenario, and not a forecast.
Reported earnings growth
FactSet's headline year-over-year S&P 500 growth rate, built from GAAP earnings. Adjusted figures are context, not the measure.
Materially
A hit of 3 percentage points or more in a quarter, unless the headline story a reasonable reader takes away is shown to be unchanged. A smaller hit also counts if it changes that story, for example by turning acceleration into deceleration.
Base effects
A decline caused only by comparison with a large year-earlier quarter is excluded.

The Case

  1. Both major holders booked Anthropic-related gains as earnings in 2026. Amazon's Q2 10-Q records $50.5B of "upward adjustments" in "Other income (expense), net" that are "related to Anthropic's fundings." Alphabet's Q2 10-Q reports $124.3B of non-marketable holdings that "primarily consist of our investment in a private company." Sources: Amazon Q2 2026 10-Q · Alphabet Q2 2026 10-Q
  2. Alphabet's private holding is very likely its Anthropic stake, but this is an inference. Alphabet does not name the company. The size is consistent with a reported 14% stake at recent round prices. After later dilution, a conservative estimate of the Anthropic portion is $88B to $106B. Source: New York Times, March 11, 2025
  3. Those gains are in the headline index growth rate. FactSet states that it used GAAP EPS for both companies in its earnings growth rate and names both gains. Q2 2026 S&P 500 growth was 52.0%, compared with 33.8% excluding Alphabet and Amazon. Source: FactSet Earnings Insight, August 28, 2026
  4. The gains were unrealized. The stakes were remeasured, not sold. Of Alphabet's $99.0B Q2 gain on equity securities, $77.4B was unrealized on the non-marketable holdings, and only $278M came from sales. Amazon's gain was a price adjustment on its holdings. An unrealized loss would run through the same income line. Sources: Alphabet Q2 2026 10-Q · Amazon Q2 2026 10-Q
  5. After an IPO, the full stakes run through profit. Amazon holds about $92.5B of Anthropic preferred stock and $97.9B of convertible notes, and its filing states that the notes convert to common stock at an IPO. Once Anthropic is listed, stakes with a readily determinable market price are marked each quarter, and the change is recognized in net income. Source: Amazon Q2 2026 10-Q
  6. A markdown before listing is unlikely. Recent valuation evidence points up. No Anthropic transaction below the $965B Series H is verified on or before September 30, 2026. The realistic path to a booked loss runs through a listing.
  7. A listing inside the window is realistic. A draft S-1 has reportedly been on file since June. Reuters reports the IPO could slip past the November 3, 2026 midterms, which is still well before Q1 2027. A listing by about Q1 2027 lets a later decline land in Q1 or Q2 2027 reporting. Amazon's one-time conversion gain lands in the IPO quarter, so it does not offset a decline in a later quarter. Source: Reuters, September 19, 2026
  8. Meaningful post-IPO declines are common. A 10% decline in a new listing is modest. Facebook fell roughly 50% within four months of its May 2012 IPO.
  9. A modest decline clears the materiality threshold. The arithmetic is below. Against a quarterly S&P 500 earnings base of about $618.3B (Q3 2025), a 10% decline costs roughly 4 points of headline growth, or about 3.5 points using the lower Alphabet estimate. Larger declines should be recomputed against the Q1 and Q2 2026 bases, which already include Anthropic gains.
  10. A hit of that size can change the headline story. Swings of about 3 points have led FactSet's headline before, as with Amazon's Rivian marks in 2022. A larger decline could turn reported acceleration into deceleration.

Conclusion

If Anthropic lists by about Q1 2027, a realistic post-listing decline can reduce FactSet's headline S&P 500 earnings growth by about 3.5 points or more in a single quarter. That meets the materiality threshold. A reduced valuation of Anthropic can therefore materially hurt reported S&P 500 earnings growth. This is a statement about capacity, not a prediction that a decline will occur.

Strongest Objections

"The index would still show strong growth." A move from about 29% to about 25% remains well above the five-year average of about 16.4%. However, 3 points is the stated threshold, swings of that size have led the headline before, and larger realistic declines would change the direction of growth, not just its level.

"FactSet discloses the gains, so readers can adjust." The claim concerns reported growth, which is the headline figure. An adjusted figure printed beside it does not change the reported number.

"Amazon's note conversion produces a gain, not a loss." That gain lands in the IPO quarter. It does not offset a decline in any later quarter.

"No markdown has happened." Agreed, and a markdown before listing is unlikely. The claim is that the mechanism exists and can realistically be triggered after a listing inside the window.

What Would Break the Thesis

  • The stakes do not run through profit after listing.
  • Alphabet's private holding is not Anthropic. Amazon alone yields about 2.4 points on a 10% decline, below the threshold, so the case would then rest on showing the story changes.
  • A sourced date places the listing after Q1 2027, leaving no realistic post-listing quarter inside the window.
